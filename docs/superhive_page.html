BLENDER 5.x ADD-ON

Spline Surface

Smooth, car-body-style surfaces, built from curves. No NURBS knowledge required.

[[IMAGE: hero render or viewport shot of the hood + fender sample with Zebra stripes]]

Draw curves, turn them into surfaces, join the surfaces smoothly, check how smooth the seams really are, and export one welded mesh. Every surface remembers how it was made, so you can change a value later instead of rebuilding.

The problem it solves

Joining two surfaces so that their positions match is easy. Making the join look smooth under reflections is not. A seam can touch perfectly and still show a crease the moment light moves across it.

Spline Surface lets you join patches at the continuity you need, shows you which seams are weak, and keeps everything editable.

G0
The edges meet. A crease is visible.
G1
The tangents match. The crease disappears.
G2
The curvature matches. Reflection stripes flow through the seam.

What you can do

1. Build surfaces from curves

  • Coons: a patch from 3 or 4 boundary curves
  • Loft: through section curves, with optional extra rows
  • Curve Network: a grid of crossing curves becomes a patch grid, G2 inside, with a checker that shows how your curves are read
  • Sweep: carry an edge shape along another edge; the straight end can stop at a plane, an edge or a curve
  • Hood, roof and door presets; blueprint planes at real size

2. Join, fill and round

  • Extend Edge, Bridge Edges, Match Edge at G0, G1 or G2
  • Corner Fill and Fill Hole
  • Fillet: round creases with a chosen radius; corners where fillets meet are filled automatically
  • Flange: fold a rounded wall off an edge, then change its angle and depth later
  • Offset Surface and Split Patch

3. Change things afterwards

  • Live Update: move a source and everything built from it follows
  • Drag a viewport arrow to change an Extend length, an Offset distance or a Fillet radius
  • Mirror Group, Cage (lattice-style bulk deformation) and Linked Edit
  • Soft Pull: drag a point, line or control point on a surface; seam-locked points stay put

4. Inspect before you trust it

  • Zebra stripes and Seam Colors (G2 green, G1 yellow, G0 red)
  • Curvature Comb and Curvature Map
  • Check Continuity: every seam listed, worst first, with a jump button
  • Panel Gap: measure gaps and steps between facing panels

Out comes a normal mesh

To Mesh welds every patch into one mesh with matching seams and mostly quads. A Curve Detail setting keeps small rounded patches (flanges, fillets) round instead of faceted. After that, rendering, modifiers and export to other software work as usual.

A typical flow: a car hood

  1. Stand up top and side blueprints at real size
  2. Trace them with curves in both directions
  3. Curve Network turns the grid of curves into a surface
  4. Extend or Bridge to the fender, matching the seam at G1 or G2
  5. Check Seam Colors and Zebra; fix weak spots with Soft Pull
  6. Fold the edge with Flange, round the crease
  7. Mirror to the other side, then To Mesh

Good to know

  • Requires Blender 5.x.
  • Patches are Blender NURBS surfaces and the final output is a mesh. It is not a manufacturing-grade CAD exporter.
  • Patches you edit by hand are marked as hand-edited and are never overwritten by Live Update.
  • Not meant for sculpting or organic characters.
  • Verification on real projects is still in progress. If something goes wrong, the add-on has a Copy Bug Report button that collects the details for you.
